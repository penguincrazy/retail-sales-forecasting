01 / RESEARCH QUESTION & SCOPE

What are we trying to forecast?

Given historical daily sales and information known at the forecast origin, how accurately can we estimate sales for each store–product-family series over the next 16 days?

PREDICTION UNIT

Store × family × date

Each observation represents one product family sold by one store on one day. The target is non-negative daily sales.

TARGET TRANSFORM

log1p(sales)

Models learn on a log-transformed target to handle the highly skewed sales distribution. Predictions are returned to the sales scale before metric calculation.

DECISION BOUNDARY

No future actual sales

Sales lags and rolling features are shifted by at least 16 days. Calendar variables are known in advance; promotions are assumed known for the forecast window.

QUALITY CHECKS

Zeros are observations, not missingness

No missing target values, duplicate train rows or negative sales were detected in the initial audit. Approximately 31.3% of training rows have zero sales; those observations are retained. The competition test is checked for duplicate keys and complete 54 × 33 coverage on all 16 dates.

03 / PREPROCESSING & FEATURES

Features built around the forecast origin.

The key safeguard is temporal: no feature can use actual sales from inside the 16-day window being predicted.

CALENDAR

Temporal structure

Day, weekday, month, week, quarter, year, weekend flags and cyclical weekday/month encodings.

SALES HISTORY

Lag and rolling features

Sales lags from 16 to 373 days, plus shifted rolling means and standard deviations over 7, 14 and 28 days.

KNOWN CONTEXT

Promotions, holidays, stores

Promotion indicators, national/regional/local holiday flags, and store/family metadata. Future oil and transaction values are excluded.

Training-only preprocessing: imputers, scalers and one-hot encoders are fitted within each training fold. The final model fitting is capped at 80,000 sampled training rows when required; all validation and holdout rows are evaluated.
04 / BASELINE & MODELS

Start with a Seasonal naive (Base model) benchmark.

The baseline retrieves the latest available value for the matching weekday at or before the forecast origin. If that history is missing, it falls back to the store-family median.

BASELINE

Seasonal naive (Base model)

For each target date, the source date is shifted backward by 7-day multiples according to its horizon position. This is a meaningful benchmark for weekly demand patterns.

CANDIDATE MODELS

Eight approaches compared

Seasonal naive (Base model), OLS, Ridge, Lasso, Decision Tree, Random Forest, KNN and LightGBM. The sklearn pipelines fit preprocessing on training data only.

05 / EVALUATION & RESULTS

LightGBM leads on RMSLE.

Three consecutive rolling-origin development folds each evaluate 16 days. The final 16 labelled days are held out separately and are not used to choose model settings.

06 / ERROR ANALYSIS & LIMITATIONS

Aggregate performance hides uneven difficulty.

School and Office Supplies is the most difficult family by holdout RMSLE (1.09808), while Books is the lowest (0.15227). Error levels differ substantially by family and sales scale.

FEATURE ABLATION

Sales history matters most

Removing sales-history features increases mean rolling RMSLE from 0.41428 to 0.58348. This is a predictive ablation, not a causal claim.

KNOWN-FUTURE INPUT

Promotion availability

The forecast assumes promotion values are known over the 16-day horizon. In a real deployment, late changes to promotion plans could alter accuracy.

GENERALISATION

Historical and sampled setup

Results describe one retailer, one historical period and an 80,000-row fitting cap. They do not guarantee future operational performance.

07 / CONCLUSION

What the experiment supports.

Among the evaluated configurations, LightGBM has the lowest mean rolling-validation RMSLE and the lowest final-holdout RMSLE. It also improves on the base model on both final-holdout metrics. The base model has lower mean rolling-validation MAE, so the preferred model depends on the metric and evaluation period. These results support LightGBM for this defined 16-day task, not a claim of universal superiority or a complete inventory policy.

Interpretation: the evidence is strongest for lower relative/log-scale error under RMSLE. The training-row cap, promotion-availability assumption, three validation folds and historical single-retailer scope remain important limitations.
