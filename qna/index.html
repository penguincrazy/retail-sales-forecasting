FORECASTING WORKFLOW

From panel checks to model evaluation.

The analysis follows a chronological forecasting design so that features and model evaluation reflect the information available before each forecast window.

01 · DATA

Daily panel validation

The training panel contains 3,000,888 observations from 1 January 2013 to 15 August 2017, covering 54 stores and 33 product families. The competition test contains 28,512 rows across 16 dates. Training and test keys were checked for duplicates, and test coverage was checked against the store and family universe.

02 · FEATURES

Forecast-time information

The target is transformed with log1p(sales). Calendar and cyclical variables, store metadata, holiday indicators and promotion counts are combined with sales lags from 16 to 373 days and rolling summaries shifted by 16 days. Actual sales inside the forecast window are never used as predictors.

03 · MODELS

Baseline and candidate models

The seasonal-naive reference captures weekly repetition. Candidate models span linear regression, regularised regression, a single tree, random forest, nearest neighbours and gradient boosting. Preprocessing is fitted only on the training portion of each fold.

04 · VALIDATION

Rolling-origin evaluation

Three successive 16-day validation windows were used: 13–28 June, 29 June–14 July and 15–30 July 2017. Model selection used mean rolling RMSLE. The final 31 July–15 August 2017 holdout was kept apart from model selection.

05 · DIAGNOSTICS

Errors and feature contribution

Evaluation includes RMSLE and MAE, family-level holdout errors, a complete store–family ranking, feature-group ablation and a forecast-day analysis based on rolling-fold predictions. These diagnostics describe model behaviour under the tested setup.

METHOD AND ASSUMPTIONS

How the forecasting experiment was configured.

The modelling choices were fixed to provide a comparable set of candidate methods under the same chronological folds and information boundary.

Seasonal naive (Base model)

For a forecast target date, the baseline selects the most recent matching weekday strictly before the forecast origin. The source date is target date minus seven times the ceiling of the lead time divided by seven. This uses a 7-, 14- or 21-day historical match for the 16-day horizon. If that store–family–date is unavailable, the method uses the store–family historical median calculated from data available at the origin; zero is the final fallback if the median is also unavailable.

LightGBM configuration

LightGBM uses a regression objective on log1p(sales), 600 estimators, learning rate 0.04, 31 leaves, minimum 30 rows per leaf, colsample_bytree 0.8 and L2 regularisation 1.0. The GPU capability probe selects GPU with max_bin 63 or CPU with max_bin 255. The settings are fixed comparison parameters rather than an exhaustive hyperparameter search.

The constructor includes subsample=0.8, but subsample_freq=0 leaves row bagging disabled. Feature subsampling remains active.

Preprocessing and compute

Numeric variables use median imputation; categorical variables use most-frequent imputation and one-hot encoding. Scale-sensitive models also use standardisation. Each pipeline is fitted within its training fold. Where needed, model fitting uses a deterministic sample of at most 80,000 training rows with seed 42; validation and holdout rows are evaluated in full.

Known-future inputs

Calendar dates are known in advance. Promotion counts supplied for the forecast dates are treated as known at the forecast origin. Future actual sales, future oil prices and future transaction counts are excluded. This promotion assumption is important: a deployment without pre-announced promotion information would need a different feature set and a new evaluation.

ERROR ANALYSIS AND FEATURE ABLATION

Performance varies across product families.

Aggregate scores are supplemented with category and series diagnostics to show where errors are concentrated.

Family-level holdout errors

School and Office Supplies has the highest family RMSLE at 1.09808. Books has the lowest at 0.15227. The full family analysis covers 864 holdout observations per family.

Feature-group ablation

The full feature set has mean rolling RMSLE 0.41428. Removing sales-history features increases it to 0.58348, the largest deterioration among the tested feature-group removals. This indicates predictive contribution under the evaluated design, not a causal effect.

Store–family audit

The final-holdout audit covers all 54 × 33 = 1,782 store–family series, each with 16 dates. It ranks series by mean absolute error and records mean actual sales, mean predictions, signed error and RMSLE.

Forecast-day analysis

The measured horizon profile is based on all three rolling-validation folds, with 1,782 observations per fold, model and forecast day. LightGBM RMSLE varies from 0.40223 on day 1 to 0.42524 on day 16, with fluctuations rather than a steady day-by-day rise; its highest mean MAE is 96.50 on day 4. The Seasonal naive (Base model) RMSLE ranges from 0.50415 on day 7 to 0.54239 on day 15, and its highest mean MAE is 108.38 on day 6. The comparison suggests some deterioration toward the later lead times, but not a monotonic increase in error. The final holdout is not used in this analysis.

LIMITATIONS AND REPRODUCIBILITY

Scope and interpretation boundaries.

The conclusions apply to the supplied retailer data, historical periods, selected features and fixed model configurations described in this report.

Data and generalisation

The experiment covers one retailer and one historical period. Performance may differ for other retailers, market regimes or future operating conditions.

Training and validation

Only three rolling folds were used, and model fitting was capped at 80,000 rows where required. Candidate hyperparameters were not exhaustively tuned.

Forecast assumptions

Promotion values are assumed known over the entire forecast window. If they are not available at prediction time, the model inputs and measured performance may change.

Uncertainty and decision use

Fold standard deviations are descriptive and are not calibrated forecast intervals. Forecast errors alone do not determine inventory decisions because stockout, spoilage and holding-cost information is not available.

Design implication: the 16-day horizon determines the lag boundary, rolling-feature shift and validation-window length. A different horizon would require rebuilding those features and aligning the rolling evaluation to the new lead time. Similarly, removing the known-promotion assumption would require excluding unavailable promotion inputs and evaluating the revised model under that information constraint.
